1. System Overview & Block Diagram

This analog frontend extracts 8 morphological features from a two-lead ECG signal for arrhythmia classification. It is designed for ultra-low-power operation at sub-threshold bias currents (~150 pA per differential pair), targeting implantable or wearable cardiac monitoring devices. The features feed directly into a ReRAM crossbar-based random forest classifier.

Signal Flow Summary

The frontend processes ECG signals through three stages:

  1. Signal conditioning: Two capacitively-coupled instrumentation amplifiers (CCIA) amplify the differential electrode signals (~1 mV) by a gain of ~100, producing amplified ECG centered at VREF = 0.3V. The CCIA topology provides built-in high-pass filtering (pseudo-resistors, fHP < 0.01 Hz) and low-pass filtering (OTA bandwidth ~570 Hz).
  2. Event detection & timing: An R-peak detector comparator identifies heartbeats. Two timing circuits then generate precisely-timed trigger pulses: a post-R delay timer (5 comparators watching a discharging capacitor) and a P-wave predictor (3 comparators watching the RR interval timer).
  3. Feature extraction: Eight analog circuits sample the ECG at specific moments relative to each heartbeat, producing 8 continuous voltage outputs that encode morphological features of each beat.

Topology: Capacitively-Coupled Instrumentation Amplifier (CCIA)

A 5-transistor OTA with capacitive feedback. Input coupling capacitors (Cin ≈ 10 pF, MIM W=L=71 μm) AC-couple the electrode signals. Feedback capacitor (Cfb ≈ 0.1 pF, MIM W=L=7 μm) sets the gain = Cin/Cfb ≈ 100. Back-to-back subthreshold PMOS pseudo-resistors (>1 TΩ) set the DC operating point to VREF without loading the OTA.

Built-in Filtering

  • High-pass: fHP = 1/(2π·Rpseudo·Cfb) ≈ 1/(2π·1012·0.1 pF) < 0.01 Hz — removes electrode DC offset and baseline wander
  • Low-pass: fLP = Gm/(2π·Cload) ≈ 148 pA/(2π·5 pF) ≈ 570 Hz — natural anti-aliasing, well above 150 Hz ECG bandwidth

4. Trigger Generation

4.1 Post-R Delay Timer delay_timer.spice

  • rpeak_pulse
  • trig_s14
  • trig_s69
  • trig_wid
  • trig_bi_copy
  • trig_rr_copy
  • vdd, vss
  • vbias_delay
  • vth_14, vth_69, vth_83, vth_300, vth_10

Topology

Main timer: a 100 pF MIM cap (W=L=224 μm) precharged to VDD at each R-peak via a PMOS switch, then discharged at constant current (~63 pA) through a vbias-controlled NFET (L=8, W=0.84). A source follower (NFET L=8 W=0.42) level-shifts the timer voltage down by ~415 mV into the 0.1–0.5V range where picoamp comparators operate correctly.

Five comparators (identical topology: L=4 W=2 NFET diff pair + L=4 W=2 PFET mirror loads, L=8 W=0.42 tail) watch the SF-shifted timer and fire when it drops below external threshold voltages.

A separate fast timer (2 pF MIM, W=L=32 μm) with its own SF generates the 10ms RR copy trigger.

4.2 P-wave Predictor pwave_predictor.spice

  • vth_base, vth_peak

Topology

Three comparators (same topology as delay timer) connected to the RR timing circuit's SF-shifted timer output. They predict when the next R-peak will occur based on the EWMA of previous RR intervals, and generate pre-R-peak trigger pulses for P-wave sampling:

  • pw_base_trig: fires at ~R−206 ms (P-wave baseline)
  • pw_peak_trig: fires at ~R−131 ms (P-wave peak)
  • rr_cap_trig: fires at ~R−5 ms (RR interval capture)

Thresholds: VTH_BASE = 0.425V, VTH_PEAK = 0.415V (calibrated for RR timer SF discharge rate of 0.113 V/s). 15 MOSFETs total.

4.3 CMOS Inverter inverter.spice

Shared utility subcircuit used by delay timer and RR timing for inverting the R-peak pulse to drive PMOS precharge switches. PFET L=0.5 W=2, NFET L=0.5 W=1. 2 MOSFETs.

5. Feature Extraction Circuits

All 8 features use only two primitive building blocks:

  • Sample-and-Hold (S&H): NFET switch (L=0.5 W=2) + MIM cap (~2 pF, W=L=32 μm). Captures ECG voltage on falling edge of trigger pulse.
  • Differential pair: NFET diff pair (L=1 W=8 or L=4 W=2) + PMOS loads (diode-connected L=4 W=2 or current mirror). Output voltage encodes the signed difference between two captured voltages. The RF classifier is invariant to monotonic transforms, so only the rank ordering of feature values across beats matters, not absolute voltages.

What it computes

  • F1 (rr_ratio): Current RR interval / EWMA of RR intervals — detects premature or delayed beats
  • F2 (rr_asym): Current RR interval / Previous RR interval — detects compensatory pauses after PVCs

Topology

A 320 pF MIM cap timer (W=L=400 μm) precharged to VDD at each R-peak, discharged at ~148 pA. A source follower (NFET L=8 W=0.42) shifts the timer voltage into the diff pair operating range (0.1–0.5V). The SF voltage at the moment of the next R-peak encodes the RR interval duration.

An EWMA filter (α = 0.1) uses charge-sharing between a 5 pF sample cap and a 45 pF EWMA cap. Two differential pairs with high-Vt PFET current mirror loads (HVT, for higher output impedance and better gain at picoamp currents) compare the current SF voltage against the EWMA and previous values.

Internal nodes timer_out and ewma_out are exposed via zero-ohm resistors for the P-wave predictor.

Critical initial conditions

.ic v(xrr.ewma_node) = 0.40
.ic v(xrr.prev_node) = 0.40
.ic v(xrr.esamp_node) = 0.40

19 MOSFETs (including inverter), 4 MIM caps. Uses pfet_01v8_hvt for diff pair loads.

5.2 F3 & F4: Beat Instability beat_instability.spice

  • ecg_in
  • capture_trig
  • copy_trig
  • instab_out
  • vdd, vss
  • vbias

What it computes

Signed difference between current and previous R-peak amplitudes. Detects beat-to-beat morphology changes (alternans, PVCs). One instance per lead (F3 = Lead 0, F4 = Lead 1).

Topology

Current R-peak sample: NFET S&H captures ECG at R-peak trigger into 5 pF cap. EWMA sample: parallel S&H into 45 pF cap. At copy_trig (R+300 ms), charge-sharing from 5 pF sample into 5 pF previous cap updates the “previous” reference. Diff pair with diode-connected PFET loads (L=4 W=2) outputs the signed difference.

8 MOSFETs, 3 MIM caps per instance. 2 instances in frontend.

5.3 F5: P-wave Signed Difference pwave_diff.spice

  • ecg_in
  • sample_base_trig
  • sample_peak_trig
  • pw_out
  • vdd, vss
  • vbias

What it computes

ECG amplitude at P-wave peak (R−131 ms) minus ECG amplitude at P-wave baseline (R−206 ms). Captures P-wave morphology without explicit P-wave detection. Sensitive to atrial fibrillation and junctional rhythms.

Topology

Two S&H circuits (each: NFET L=0.5 W=2 + 2 pF MIM cap W=L=32) capture ECG at baseline and peak times. Diff pair (NFET L=1 W=8 + PFET mirror L=1 W=4) outputs the signed difference.

7 MOSFETs, 2 MIM caps.

5.4 F6: QRS Width qrs_width.spice

  • ecg_in
  • sample_trig
  • width_out
  • vdd, vss

What it computes

ECG amplitude at R+28 ms (DELAY_SAMPLES=10 at 360 Hz). For narrow QRS complexes, the signal has returned to baseline by this point. For wide QRS (bundle branch block, PVCs), the signal is still elevated. A single voltage captures QRS duration information.

Topology

Simplest possible circuit: one NFET S&H switch (L=0.5, W=2) and one 2 pF MIM hold capacitor (W=L=32). No diff pair needed — the absolute voltage is the feature.

1 MOSFET, 1 MIM cap. No bias current (passive S&H).

5.5 F7: QRS Slope qrs_slope.spice

  • ecg_in
  • sample14_trig
  • sample69_trig
  • slope_out
  • vdd, vss
  • vbias

What it computes

ECG at R+14 ms minus ECG at R+69 ms. Captures the post-R-peak descent slope. Normal beats have a steep descent (large difference); PVCs have a slow, broad descent (small difference).

Topology

Two S&H + diode-loaded diff pair. Same architecture as pwave_diff. 7 MOSFETs, 2 MIM caps.

5.6 F8: QRS Symmetry qrs_symmetry.spice

  • ecg_in
  • sample_r_trig
  • sym_out
  • vdd, vss

What it computes

ECG amplitude at the R-peak moment. Normal sinus beats have tall, sharp R-peaks; PVCs and aberrant conduction produce lower, broader R-peaks. The absolute R-peak amplitude is a discriminative feature.

Topology

Identical to QRS width: single NFET S&H + MIM cap. 1 MOSFET, 1 MIM cap. Passive (no bias).

Results analysis

6 of 8 features achieve |ρ| > 0.86 across all records, confirming near-perfect rank preservation between the analog circuits and their Python equivalents. F5 (P-wave), F6 (width), F7 (slope), and F8 (symmetry) all achieve |ρ| > 0.97 — the passive S&H and diff pair circuits faithfully reproduce the sampled ECG values.

*F2 (rr_asym) on record 109: The low correlation (0.18) on record 109 is expected. Record 109 has extremely regular sinus rhythm (all RR intervals within ±2%), so both Python and circuit asymmetry values cluster near 1.0. With no real variation, the rank ordering is dominated by circuit noise. On record 200 (which contains PVCs where asymmetry matters), F2 achieves ρ = 0.93. The classifier does not need this feature to discriminate on record 109 since there are no arrhythmias present.

F3 (bi_L0) on record 100 shows ρ = 0.60. Record 100 contains only normal beats, so beat-to-beat amplitude differences are very small (~5 mV circuit dynamic range). On records with actual morphological variation (200: PVCs, 109: LBBB), F3 achieves ρ > 0.98.

Negative correlations (e.g., F1 = −0.99, F5 = −0.99) are expected: the diff pair topology inverts the sign of the feature. Since the RF classifier is invariant to monotonic transforms (including inversion), |ρ| is the relevant metric.

10. Filtering & Signal Conditioning

Why no dedicated HP/LP/notch filter blocks?

The CCIA topology inherently provides both high-pass and low-pass filtering, eliminating the need for separate filter circuits:

High-Pass Filtering (HPF)

The pseudo-resistors (back-to-back subthreshold PMOS, R > 1012 Ω) combined with the feedback capacitor (Cfb = 0.1 pF) form a first-order HPF with corner frequency:

f_HP = 1 / (2*pi * R_pseudo * C_fb)
     = 1 / (2*pi * 10^12 * 0.1e-12)
     < 0.01 Hz

This removes electrode DC offset and baseline wander (equivalent to a traditional 0.05 Hz HPF in clinical ECG systems) while passing all ECG frequency content.

Low-Pass Filtering (LPF)

The OTA's finite transconductance acts as a natural first-order LPF:

f_LP = Gm / (2*pi * C_load)
     = 148 pA / (2*pi * 5 pF)
     ≈ 570 Hz

This is above the clinical ECG bandwidth (<150 Hz) but provides anti-aliasing for the S&H sample points. The S&H circuits themselves also act as a form of LPF since they integrate the signal over the switch-on time (~0.5 ms).

Power-Line Rejection

The differential input topology provides common-mode rejection of 50/60 Hz power-line interference. A dedicated notch filter is not needed because:

  • CMRR of the CCIA rejects common-mode interference
  • The classifier operates on morphological amplitude features, not frequency content
  • Any residual 50/60 Hz component is deterministic and consistent beat-to-beat, so it does not affect rank ordering of features

Additional filtering in the signal chain

The MIM hold capacitors in each S&H circuit act as voltage-domain filters — they capture the instantaneous ECG voltage at a precise moment and hold it, rejecting all noise outside the sample window. This is equivalent to a sinc-function anti-aliasing filter with bandwidth inversely proportional to the sample pulse width.
